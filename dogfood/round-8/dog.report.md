### 1. The finished dog

A 0.5 × 1.13 × 1.35 unit blocky dog (a block is a unit, every part a `pbox(x0, y0, z0, w, h, d)` box on the 16-px lattice), standing on four cream paws at y = 0, facing +z. Body (8×6×8 px) with a cream chest/belly decal; four 3-px legs on hip/shoulder joints `leg_fl/fr/bl/br` at 5.5 px, each carrying a 2-px paw a pixel forward; a neck, 6-px skull and muzzle on joint `head` at the neck base; two ears on `ear_l`/`ear_r`; a 2-px tail bar tilted up 35° on `tail` at the rump; everything under root joint `body` at (0, 0.5, 0). Decals: two eyes, a nose, a red collar band round the neck and a brass tag under it. Materials: tan coat, cream, red, brass, near-black. Poses `rest, sit, beg, walk_a..d, look_up, wag_l, wag_r`; animations `walk` (loop, ease 1, ease_ends 0), `wag` (tail alone, loop), `sit_down` and `beg` (one-shots, ease 1). 18 asserts, all via anchors and `pose=`. The tail wag: the joint is declared without `axis=`, so a pose gives it the full Euler triple; up/down is the x component (`tail=[-10,0,0]` in sit, `[30,0,0]` in beg) and the sideways wag is the y component (`tail=[0, 20, 0]` / `[0, -20, 0]` in walk_a/c, ±30 in wag_l/r). It came out essentially as planned; the only departures are 1.35 long rather than 1.2 (the tail tip and muzzle add up), and the eyes/nose ended up square texels rather than round spots, forced by the Bedrock texture (see friction).

### 3. Friction

- **Decals smaller than a texel vanished from the Bedrock texture, silently.** `eye_l = sphere(0.035) | move(1.8 * px, 14 * px, 10 * px)` (and eye_r) painted the sheet and the beauty render but `model.geo.png` had no dark texel in the skull window; the region (0.56 px radius, centred between texel centres) caught no texel centre. Only a texel-by-texel read of a 64×32 PNG found it. Worked around with `eye_l = pbox(1, 13, 9, 1, 1, 2)`: whole-texel box regions. The language doc says a decal "reaches" the face texture; it should say a region must cover a texel centre (be about a pixel across, on the lattice), and the render should warn when a decal region paints no texel.
- **A decal region touching a second face paints it too, and nothing says so.** `nose = pbox(-1, 12, 12, 2, 1, 2)` with its top at y = 13 exactly on the muzzle's top face put two black texels on the muzzle's *up* window and a black bar on the top in the sheet's TOP view. Fixed with `nose = pbox(-1, 11, 12, 2, 1, 2)`. Same root cause as the earlier spherical nose spilling onto the muzzle top in `beauty.png`.
- **A decal whose region misses the shape paints nothing and nothing says so.** My first `tag_region = sphere(0.045) | move(0, 6.8 * px, 4 * px)` sat on the body's front face but the decal was applied to `head_part`; the tag was absent from the first sheet and `check` said "no warnings". A warning like "decal region 'tag_region' touches none of the shape's surface" would have saved a render.
- **`output:` and the top step's box in `check --pose` are misleading.** In `--pose sit`, `dog 0.5 × 1.84 × 1.94 x -0.25..0.25 y -0.4..1.44` and `output: dog 0.5 × 1.84 × 1.94`, while the `posed` line just above says `0.5 × 1.18 × 1.29 y 0..1.18`. The doc explains rest-box-plus-posed-line for inner steps, but the summary `output:` line should quote the posed extent, not a turned-box bound that reaches 0.4 below the floor.
- **`posed` lines carry no step name in the column.** Grepping `check --pose` output for `posed` gives 40 anonymous lines; each is indented under its step, which is fine reading the whole dump but not for reading one part. A `posed  leg_fl ...` prefix would help.
- **Per-pose asserts on part bounds: the doc left me unsure which query is posed.** language.md says "anything computed from a joint's shape (its bounds…) follows the pose" and also "a step built at rest and turned by a joint above it keeps its rest box". I avoided the question by anchoring the sole of every paw and reading `at(dog, "sole_fl")[1]`, which is clearly stated to be posed and worked. The guide's assert examples use `clearance`; one example with `at()` and `pose=` (which is what a foot-on-floor promise needs) would have gone straight in. Also, `bottom(paw_fl)` for "every paw bottom is at y = 0" is what the task suggested; I did not trust it in a pose and the doc does not settle it.
- **Nothing in the docs says how to make a rig stand on lowered legs in a pose.** The `L(1 - cos θ)` rule is there, but for sit/beg I hand-computed body pivot heights three times and still needed `check --pose` to converge (`0.6583 > 0.8166` → `0.7317 > 0.7877` → `0.7585 > 0.7663`). An `--pose` line that prints each anchor's posed point at the top (it does, under `dog`'s `anchors`), plus a doc sentence "read the anchors line under the output step in `check --pose`", would have shortened it: I found that line by accident.
- **The tail's wag axis is answered only by inference.** Task and instinct say a tail joint is "about x"; the doc's `axis=` option would have locked it to one axis, so I had to notice that a plain joint takes `[x, y, z]` and put the wag in y. A sentence in the rig section — "a tail that wags and lifts wants a plain joint: x for up, y for the wag" — would remove the doubt.
- **The `Z = 0` slice draws the rear paws' front faces as solid black rectangles** (their face lies exactly on z = 0). The doc warns about faces on sample planes for watertightness, not about black fills in a slice; I read it as a hole for a moment.
- **Regions appear as steps.** `steps.png` draws `eye_l`, `nose`, `collar_region`, `tag_region` as shapes (33 named shapes) and the report lists them "In output: yes". Harmless but they are not geometry; a "region" tag would be clearer.
- **The quick sheet shows a decal as a ragged blob** (eyes at cell 0.021 were irregular black patches), so I shrank the eyes for the wrong reason before the full render showed clean edges; the doc's "judge fine patterns in the beauty render" covers it but the quick sheet could say "decals are per-vertex here".
- **`check` exit status is lost under a pipe**, my fault, but `check` printing `asserts: 16 pass, 1 fail` without a trailing "FAILED" word means grep-filtered output does not shout.
- Speed: full render 18–19 s, the focus render 26 s (poses 4.8 s, four strips ~12 s of it); `check` 2 s; quick 3 s. Acceptable; the strips are the slow part on a rig with four animations.
- The Bedrock row and `set minecraft_entity` docs were adequate: "an entity faces north … cubes and pivots keep their x and have their z negated" matched the file exactly (pivot z −2.5 for +2.5). What the docs do not say: the 35° tail becomes 5 staircase cubes, and a rotated part in general multiplies cubes — a note "keep entity parts axis-aligned; a tilted bar becomes a stair of cubes" would have made me build the tail flat with a rest angle in a pose instead.

### 4. What worked well

`pbox` on the pixel lattice plus `set minecraft 16` gave a clean 20-cube export first time, with the bone tree exactly the joint tree and the pivots right. The joint tree printed by `check` and the report caught nothing wrong because it was easy to read. Poses, strips and the bar captions ("LOOP EASE 1 ENDS 0", "ONCE") were right first time and told me what I was looking at. The `pose=` assert form and its failure text with both sides evaluated made the beg pose converge without pictures. The unknown-pose warning lists the poses. The focus close-up with the faint context and its caption is exactly what a head needs. The quick sheet at 3 s and full at 19 s kept the loop tight. `--out` for side renders never clobbered the main one. The viewer page lists every animation with loop/speed/scrub.

### 5. Three changes that would have helped most

1. Warn (in `check` and the render) when a decal region touches no surface of the shape it is applied to, and, for the Bedrock export, when a decal paints no texel of `model.geo.png`; document that a region must cover a texel centre.
2. In `check --pose`, make the `output:` line and the output step's box the posed extent (or print both, labelled), and prefix each `posed` line with its step name.
3. In the rig section, one worked foot-on-floor example: anchor the sole, `assert abs(at(rig, "sole")[1]) < 0.01, pose=walk_a`, plus the sentence that `at()` is posed while `bottom()`/`top()` of an inner step are not (or are — say which), and that a plain joint's `[x, y, z]` is how a tail both lifts and wags.
