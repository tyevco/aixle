## 1. The finished drone

0.38 × 0.132 × 0.258 units, standing on two skid rails on y = 0, nose to +z, grid 200 (cell 0.002). A rounded-box shell with a battery hump at the back and a nose block; four tube arms to rounded pods with an iron motor and a two-blade rotor each (front pods 0.02 higher than the rear so a folded front rotor clears the rear one); the front-left pod has a red ring, the front-right a green one; a pan post under the nose carries a yoke and a camera box with an iron lens ring and a glass lens. Materials: `material("#3a3a3e", rough=0.9)` shell, near-black rotors, iron, red, green, glass. Rig: root joint `body` at (0, 0.085, 0) containing hinge joints `arm_fl`/`arm_fr` (about y at the body edge) each containing an `axis=[0,1,0]` joint `rotor_fl`/`rotor_fr`; `rotor_bl`/`rotor_br` on fixed rear arms; `gear_l`/`gear_r` about z at the body underside; `pan` about y under the nose containing `tilt` about x at the camera centre. Poses: folded, gear_up, takeoff, bank, look_down, spin, spin_120/240/360 (from a `def spin_pose`), hover_a/b, pan_left/right, tilt_down. Animations: spin_up (loop, 0.6 s, four keys through 360°, fl/br one way and fr/bl the other), launch (loop=0, times=[0,1,1.6,3], ease=1: folded → rest → gear_up → takeoff), hover (loop, ease=1, body bob ±0.03 with pan ±20° and tilt drifting), gimbal (loop, ease=1: rest → pan_left → pan_right → tilt_down → rest). Everything in the plan was built; the only deviations are additions: blade-tip anchors and `at()` steps used to verify the rotor directions numerically, and a small `gear_up` pose as the launch's third key. Final report: watertight yes, one piece, stands, no warnings.

## 3. Friction

- `check` sizes at two decimals, report at three, at a 0.4-unit model. `lens = cylinder(0.006, 0.006)` printed as `lens 0.01 × 0.01 × 0.01`; `by = 0.085` printed `by = 0.09`; `cam_y = 0.036` as `0.04`; volume 0.001 for both original and re-import. I could not compare sizes below the third decimal without `report.json`. Worked around by reading `report.json` with node. The task fixed the scale at 0.4, but the skill also says "1 to 20 units" with no warning that precision degrades below that.
- Steps built inside a `def` have no name, so no `posed` line and no thumbnail. First version had `def rotor(px,py,pz,name) = joint(..., name, ...)`; `check --pose folded` printed nothing for any rotor. Expected: something for the joint. Worked around by moving the `joint(...)` out to named steps `rotor_fl = joint(rotor(...), "rotor_fl", ...)`. Docs mention the inline-def limit only under `--focus`.
- A joint step the pose itself turns gets no `posed` line for the joints above it. `check --pose takeoff`: `gear_l ... surface 0.08 × 0.02 × 0.16 x -0.11..-0.04 y 0.06..0.08` — the gear's own swing but not the body's +0.25 lift; it is really at y 0.31..0.33. `rotor_fl` and `camera` (not posed themselves) did get `posed y 0.37..0.38`. Same in `bank`. Not worked around; noted.
- `at()` on a nested joint's own step ignores the joints above it, and the docs ("in a pose, at() on a joint's subtree is the posed point") do not say which frame. `tip_fl = at(rotor_fl, "tip")` in `--pose folded` printed `[-0.07, 0.13, 0.11]` (the rest point) while `rotor_fl`'s posed box was `x -0.1..-0.08 z -0.09..0.03`. Worked around with `tip_fl_world = at(arm_fl, "tip")`, which gave `[-0.09, 0.13, 0.03]`.
- `poses.png` and `anim_<name>.png` ignore `--focus`. `render --focus pan` (with strips kept) produced `anim_gimbal.png` and `poses.png` framing the whole drone; the gimbal is ~10 px and its 50° pans and 70° tilt are invisible; the same for `look_down`, `pan_left`, `pan_right`, `tilt_down` thumbnails. Gave up on judging the gimbal animation from the strip; judged one pose with `--pose look_down --focus pan`.
- A small root `xform(move=...)` is invisible on a strip. `hover_a ... body=xform(move=[0, 0.265, 0])` vs takeoff's 0.25: eight identical frames. Worked around by making the bob 0.03; the strip has no numbers (e.g. the root's y) to read instead.
- "Would tip over" warning on a flying pose. `render --pose takeoff` warned "The centre of mass (0, 0.337, 0.001) is 0.063 units outside the base's footprint: the model would tip over" — the pose lifts the model 0.25 off y = 0. Expected: no standing judgement for a pose whose root leaves the floor, or a note that it is the pose. Ignored.
- The report's Joints row is a flat list ("body at (…); arm_fl at (…); rotor_fl at (…)…") and the `exports:` line says only "11 meshes in 12 nodes"; neither says which joint is inside which. To confirm nesting I parsed the GLB JSON by hand with node.
- Skid struts left 7–8 non-manifold edges "in 'gear_r' alone (no other part within a cell: a crease of its own surface, or the mesher's noise)". Lines: `tube(0.005, [x, 0.07, 0.05, x + s * 0.012, 0.006, 0.05])` meeting `tube(0.006, [... 0.006 ...])`. Thickening the rail to 0.0075 and ending the struts with `cap="flat"` still left 7; struts at 0.0065 (3.4 cells) fixed it. The message blamed the strut alone, and the doc's "about three cells" for a clean line is under rigging lines, not struts.
- Slice label rounding: `slices.png` says "Y = 0.07" but the cut was at 0.066 (the bounds' centre) and missed the rear arms (y 0.067..0.083), so the Y slice showed only two pod circles and no arms, which read as missing arms until I did the arithmetic.
- `--focus pan` clips the body to the frame and draws it as a huge flat slab above the gimbal ("DRONE → PAN" sheet); the first reading was of an unknown plate part until I remembered the clip. A clipped neighbour could be drawn ghosted or the clip stated in the caption.
- The strip bar says `EASE 1` and the `times=` seconds but not `loop=0`; the launch bar reads the same as a looping one.
- Speed: full render 24–26 s at grid 200, of which poses 5.7 s and the four strips 10.5 s (timings in report.md); `--no-poses --no-export --no-viewer` for pose checks brought it to ~8 s. Quick render 3.5 s. `viewer.html` is 4.3 MB (GLB embedded).
- Doc gaps I hit: nothing says an axis joint's pose value is a bare number *in a def call* (`def spin_pose(name, a) = pose(name, rotor_fl=a, ...)` worked, but I had to assign the result `s1 = spin_pose(...)` since I did not know whether a bare call is a statement); nothing says that a 360° loop needs intermediate keys (I assumed the GLB's quaternion slerp takes the short way and used 120° steps; not verified in a viewer); the reference lists `xform(rotate=0, move=0, scale=1)` with defaults of 0 that are not the shape of the values.
- The viewer page could not be loaded here (no three.js). From its text it lists: Perspective/Front/Right/Top, Wireframe, Grid, ▶ spin_up, ▶ launch, ▶ hover, ▶ gimbal, ▶ All, ❚❚ pause, a loop checkbox (checked), a speed select ¼×/½×/1×/2×, and a scrub range slider.

## 4. What worked well

`joint(part, name, x, y, z)` with a world pivot after the part is in place, nesting by union (`arm(...) + rotor_fl` then `joint(..., "arm_fl", ...)`): the folded rotor followed its arm first time. `axis=[0,1,0]` with a single number per pose. `xform(rotate=, move=)` on the root for takeoff and bank. `times=` and `ease=1`, and the strip bar naming both with each key's second. `check --pose` `posed`/`surface` lines for the folded arms. `poses.png` ordering (rest first) and the strips' eight frames were enough to judge every body-scale pose without a full render. Pose via a `def` for the spin thirds. The quick render's warning naming the five steps thinner than its cell. The watertight diagnostics with position, step and count, which found the strut seam. Anchors plus `at()` as a numeric check of rotor direction. The re-import round trip and its resolution warning. The beauty render was presentable on the second try (ambient 1.4, zoom 1.15).

## 5. The three changes that would have helped most

1. Scale the printed precision to the model (significant figures rather than fixed decimals) in `check`, `report.md` and the slice labels, and give every step under a posed joint a `posed` line, including a joint step the pose turns itself (gear_l in takeoff).
2. Make `--focus` (and `set focus`) apply to `poses.png` and the animation strips, or add a focused strip, so a small joint's animation (a gimbal, a hand) can be judged; and print the root's/focused joint's posed position on each strip frame so a small `xform` bob is readable.
3. Show the joint tree, indented, in the report's Joints row and after the `exports:` line, and document `at()` on a nested joint's step (it is posed by that joint only; read it through the outer joint for the world point), that steps inside a `def` get no posed line or thumbnail, and that a 360° rotor loop needs keys under 180° apart.
