## 1. The finished toy

A 1 × 1 × 1 hollow oak box (walls 0.06, `round=0.03`) standing on y = 0, painted with `decal` regions: a red/cream checker panel on the front (+z) and on the lid's top, blue/cream horizontal stripes on both sides, green dots on the back. A brass hinge barrel runs along the back top edge and a brass boss sits on the right wall. The lid is a 0.12 slab overlapping the body's top by 0.02, with a brass knob at its front, declared `joint(lid_part, "lid", 0, 1.0, -0.5, axis=[1, 0, 0])`; closed height with the knob is 1.18. The crank is an iron shaft along x through the boss, an iron arm up, a walnut handle back out along x and a knob, `joint(crank_part, "crank", 0.58, 0.5, 0, axis=[1, 0, 0])`. Inside, a steel `tube(0.02, helix(0.11, 0.35, 4, per_turn=24))` spring rises from the floor (its first coil sunk into the floor slab) to a twelve-point `star` ruff extruded as a collar, a neck, a head with decal eyes and a decal smile, a red nose sphere, a yellow brim, a blue/yellow striped cone hat and a pompom; two red tube arms with white glove spheres hang from the shoulders as `joint(..., "right_arm"/"left_arm", ±0.15, 0.42, 0, axis=[0, 0, 1])`, nested inside `joint(clown_part, "clown", 0, 0.06, 0)` at the floor. Poses: `shut` (rest), `ajar` (lid −25, clown moved up 0.12), `pop` (lid −115, clown `xform(move=[0, 0.04, 0], scale=[1, 2.1, 1])`, arms ±60), `pop_up` (a slightly higher `pop` for the bob), `wound` (crank 180), and `crank_q1`/`crank_q3`/`crank_full` (90/270/360) as keys for the wind loop. Animations: `wind` loop over 2 s through the five crank keys; `pop` one-shot (`loop=0`, `ease=1`, `times=[0, 0.6, 0.75, 0.95]` over `shut, shut, ajar, pop`); `bob` loop `pop → pop_up → pop` over 1 s with `ease=1`. It came out as planned; the one departure is that the `pop` stretch scales the whole clown subtree about the floor pivot, so the head becomes an egg (an acceptable squash-and-stretch look, but not a "spring stretches, clown rides up" one; see friction).

## 3. Friction log

- `assert tall(jack) < 1.3, "closed it is about 1.2 tall"` (original line 89). Expected: a promise about the closed toy. Happened: `check --pose ajar` printed `assert (line 89) fails: tall(jack) < 1.3 is 1.586 < 1.3` and `--pose pop` `2.089 < 1.3`. The language doc does say asserts run in the pose, but there is no way to say "at rest only", so a closed-height promise cannot be written for a rig. Worked around by weakening it to `assert tall(jack) < 2.3`.
- `pose("pop", ..., clown=xform(move=[0, 0.3, 0], scale=[1, 1.9, 1]))` (line 77, first version). Expected: raise and stretch. Happened: `check --pose pop` showed the spring's posed box starting at y 0.28, i.e. the whole clown floated 0.28 above the floor, since `scale` about the floor pivot already lifts everything and `move` adds on top. No warning (pieces are not judged by `check`). Worked around by `move=[0, 0.04, 0]` and `scale=[1, 2.1, 1]`. Doc gap: the Rigs section shows `xform` on a `body` but never on a part that must stay attached at its pivot.
- Same lines: `scale=[1, 2.1, 1]` scales the head, hat and nested arm joints too (egg head). What a jack-in-the-box wants is the spring stretched and the clown translated by the spring's growth. `angle("clown")` reads only angles, so a nested clown joint cannot read its parent's pose scale or move to compensate. Gave up and accepted the stretched head.
- `joint(lid_part, "lid", 0, 1.0, -0.5, axis=[1, 0, 0])` / `pose("ajar", lid=-25, ...)` (lines 41, 76). Expected the docs to say which sign opens the lid. Neither `language.md` nor `reference.md` states the sign convention for an `axis=` angle; I inferred right-handed about the axis direction from the `rotate` rule and confirmed with `check --pose` posed lines (positive 90 about [1,0,0] takes the crank arm from +y to +z; the lid on a back hinge with its front at +z opens with a negative angle). Nothing says whether `axis=[-1,0,0]` flips the sign.

## 4. What worked well enough that I would not change it

`check` in about a second with every step's box, and `check --pose` with `posed`/`surface` lines: it answered every sign question (lid, crank, arms) before a single render. The 2-pieces warning with the loose piece's volume, centre and steps pointed straight at the 0.015 gap. `decal` for the panels, eyes and smile, `extrude(star(...))` for the ruff, `helix` into `tube` for the spring, `material(preset, scale=)` for the wood: all first time. Axis joints took one angle and exported as clean quaternions; `times=` and `ease=1` did exactly what the docs said and the strip bar printed the times. `--pose x --focus part --no-poses --no-export --out dir` gave a 13 s close-up without disturbing the main folder. The pose sheet's rest-first row and the beauty render needed no tuning. The re-import round trip preserved volume, pieces and the cavity.

## 5. The three changes that would have helped most

1. Make `--quick` cheap again for rigs: skip (or mesh at the quick cell and share frames between identical poses) the pose sheet and strips under `--quick`, and mesh each unique pose once at full grid; have the skill say `--quick --no-poses` while iterating geometry. 60 of my 82 s per full render and 12 of 15 s per quick pass went to sheets I read once.
2. State the `axis=` sign convention in the Rigs section with a hinged lid and a crank as the examples (right-handed about the axis direction; a lid on a back hinge opens with a negative angle about +x), and add "a pose's `move` adds after `scale` about the pivot, so a part that must stay on its pivot wants scale alone" with a spring-mounted example.
3. A rest-only assert (or `assert ... at "shut"`), and a pose-reading query beyond `angle()` (the parent's `move`/`scale`) so a nested joint can ride a scaled parent; failing that, a `--focus` for `poses.png`/strips so a small joint like a crank can be judged without a full-size render per pose.
