## 1. The finished figure

A 0.9 × 2.5 × 0.5 stud wooden marionette standing on y = 0 facing +z: a rounded pine chest and pelvis, a pine neck and a skin-coloured sphere head with decal eyes, mouth and pink cheeks; two arms (capsule upper arm, capsule forearm, a flat paddle hand) and two legs (capsule thigh, capsule shin, a rounded-box foot pointing +z), every hinge marked by a walnut ball (shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, ankle, neck). Ten joints: root `body` at the origin (so `xform(scale=)` squashes against the floor), `neck`, `left_shoulder` > `left_elbow`, `right_shoulder` > `right_elbow`, `left_hip` > `left_knee`, `right_hip` > `right_knee`; arms and legs come from two `def`s (`arm(shoulder, elbow, s)`, `leg(hip, knee, s)`) that take the joint names as strings and nest the inner `joint` inside the outer's part. Poses: `stand`, `wave` (left arm up sideways, forearm bent up), `step_left`, `step_right` (30° front leg with a 20° knee so the foot stays flat, 25°/35° trailing leg, opposite arm forward, body dropped 0.08 so the front foot lands), `crouch` (hips -45, knees 80, arms forward, `body=xform(move=[0,-0.13,0], scale=[1.06,0.94,1.06])`), `jump` (legs straight, V arms, `body=xform(move=[0,0.5,0], scale=[0.94,1.1,0.94])`). Animations: `walk` (step_left → step_right → step_left, 1.2 s, loop, ease=1), `wave` (stand → wave → stand, `times=[0, 0.25, 1.2]`, ease=1: up fast, down slowly), `jump` (crouch → jump → stand, `times=[0, 0.3, 0.9]`, loop=0, ease=1). `set roblox 1` and `anchor("BodyBackAttachment", 0, 1.6, -0.15)` on the root. It came out as planned; the two things that needed a second pass were my pose numbers (the wave's forearm, the walk's floor contact), not the rig.

## 3. Friction log

- Line `assert tall(marionette) < 2.6, "about 2.5 studs tall"`: expected a rest-size promise; `check --pose wave` and `--pose jump` failed it ("tall(marionette) < 2.6 is 2.721 < 2.6", "3.067 < 2.6"). Documented that asserts run in the pose, but there is no way to assert a rest-only size (or a per-pose one) on a rig, so I replaced it with the back-accessory height limit 8.59, which is nearly meaningless for a 2.5-stud figure. A `rest=1` flag on `assert`, or an `at_rest(expr)`, would fix it.
- `pose("step_left", left_hip=[-32,0,0], right_hip=[28,0,0], right_knee=[45,0,0], ...)` (first version): expected the front foot on the floor; `check --pose` said "the surface floats: its lowest point is at y = 0.14". A straight leg swung forward lifts its foot; I had to work out the drop by hand from thigh/shin lengths and cosines and then add `body=xform(move=[0,-0.08,0])` and a 20° front knee. The docs' rig sections have no walk example; a sentence saying "a leg swung θ lifts its foot L(1-cos θ); drop the root joint by that, or bend the knee" would have saved a round.
- `pose("wave", left_shoulder=[0, 0, 160], left_elbow=[0, 0, -50], ...)`: expected a hand up beside the head; the thumbnail showed the forearm sticking out horizontally. The docs say nested angles are relative to the parent, but never state the sign convention in limb terms: which z sign lifts a hanging left arm outward (positive z, because `rotate(z=+θ)` takes -y towards +x), and that a hanging limb swings forward (+z) with a *negative* x. I derived both from the `rotate` sentence and put the derivation in a comment; a "for a hanging limb" line in the rig section would remove the guessing.
- `check` (no `--pose`) prints "poses: stand, wave, ..." but nothing about joints: not their names, pivots or nesting. The report.md "Assembly" row does (after a full render), and `explain` shows a `joint "neck" at (0, 1.9, 0)` tag per named step, but neither shows the joints created inside a `def` (`left_elbow`, `left_knee`) or the tree of which joint is under which; I verified the nesting by parsing the GLB's node array with a Python script. `explain`'s top line for `marionette` also lacks the `joint "body"` tag (the `| anchor(...)` on the outside seems to hide it). A joint tree in `check` and `explain` ("body > left_shoulder > left_elbow ...") would let an agent confirm the rig without rendering.
- `poses.png` title bar reads "10 JOINTS: BODY, NECK, LEFT_SHOULDER, LEFT_ELBOW, RIGHT_SHOULDER, RIGHT_ELBOW, ..." and truncates the rest; the four leg joints are never named on any picture.
- `anchor("BodyBackAttachment", 0, 1.6, -0.15)`: I guessed the name from the task. The language doc lists three limits (hat, face, back) but never the attachment names the tool recognises or how it maps a name to a limit; the report answered "fits the back limit", so the mapping exists but is undocumented. A table (HatAttachment, FaceFrontAttachment, BodyBackAttachment, RightShoulderAttachment ... with each limit) belongs in the Roblox paragraph.
- The Roblox row's budget: the docs say a jointed model "is an animated rig for Studio rather than a rigid accessory", yet because there is an Attachment anchor the 4000-triangle accessory budget applied, shared across 10 meshes (~400 each), and there is no word about whether that is what a rig needs or how to ask for the 10000-per-mesh budget instead (drop the anchor? then the attachment node is gone too). Not an error, but I could not tell which budget a rigged back accessory should get.
- `exports: 10 meshes in 11 nodes, 39624 triangles` versus the sheet's `30244 TRIS`: nothing says why the export has 30% more triangles (each joint's part is meshed on its own and the overlaps at every hinge are kept). Documenting it would stop an agent chasing the difference.
- The `--pose step_left` report says "Watertight: no: 14 edges ... in 'chest', 'left_arm'" and "Stands: no": in a pose these are properties of the pose, not the model, and there is no note saying the exports (at rest) are unaffected. A rig's posed report should say "in pose step_left" on those rows.
- `animation("walk", [...], seconds=1.2, loop=1, ease=1)`: ease=1 slows to a stop at *every* key including the two extremes of a loop, so the walk strip settles at each step rather than cycling; that is what the brief asked for and what the docs say, but a looping gait wants no stop at its keys. An `ease` that eases only the ends of a one-shot, or a per-key list, would help; the docs could at least say a looping cycle usually wants `ease=0` or a low value.
- `import("../out/marionette/model.glb")` warns "sampled at cell 0.026, coarser than this render's 0.02 ... Give import() resolution=128 to match": helpful, but the default resolution 96 is lower than the default grid 128, so the warning appears on every default re-import; the default could just follow the grid.
- Decal steps (`eye_l`, `eye_r`, `mouth`, `cheeks`) are listed in the report's Steps table as "In output: yes" and drawn as solid geometry on `steps.png`, though the language doc says a region-only step "is not geometry". Harmless but contradicts the doc; a "region" tag would be clearer.
- On the quick jump sheet the eye decals read as an angry face (per-vertex colouring at cell 0.048); the docs do say to judge decals in beauty, and the beauty render was fine. Noting it because at first glance it looked like a different face.
- Timing: full render 19–20 s (poses 2.3 s, three strips 7.6 s, hierarchy + roblox 3.8 s); pose sheets with `--no-export --no-poses` 6 s; quick 6 s; check 2 s. All acceptable. The `Write` tool was needed for the program (heredocs are refused), and Python replacements for edits.

## 4. What worked well

- `joint(part, name, x, y, z)` with the world pivot after building the part in place, nested by simply putting the inner `joint(...)` in the outer's `+` expression, and passing joint names as `def` string parameters: the whole rig was right the first time, no part swung about a wrong point.
- `check --pose name` with the "surface floats / lowest point is at y = ..." notes: this is what caught both pose errors, in two seconds, before any picture.
- `xform(move=, scale=)` on a root joint at the origin for crouch and jump: exactly as documented, the scale is about the pivot so the feet stayed on the floor.
- `times=` and `ease=` with the strip's bar restating the keyframe times: the wave strip proved the asymmetric timing at a glance.
- `--pose name --out dir --no-export --no-poses` for a full-size pose sheet without clobbering the main render; `--beauty` in a pose for a presentation picture.
- The Roblox row: one line with budget, decimation source, the attachment point, the limit it fits and the half turn; the re-import confirmed all of it.
- The report's Assembly section (joint pivots, poses, animation key lists) and the GLB node/animation structure matched the program exactly.

## 5. The three changes that would have helped most

1. A joint tree in `check` and `explain` (every joint including those made inside `def`s, its pivot, its parent), so a rig can be verified without a render or a GLB parser, and the full list on `poses.png`.
2. A "hanging limb" paragraph in the rig docs with worked walk numbers: which sign of x swings forward, which sign of z lifts an arm outward on each side, and that a straight leg swung θ lifts its foot L(1-cos θ) so the root needs `xform(move=)` or the knee a bend; plus a way to assert a rest-only size (`assert ..., rest=1`).
3. A Roblox attachment table (recognised `*Attachment` names → the limit each is checked against) and a sentence on which triangle budget a *jointed* accessory gets and why, next to a note that the export's triangle count exceeds the sheet's because each joint's part is meshed separately.
