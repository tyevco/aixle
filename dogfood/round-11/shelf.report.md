# Round 11: a parametric bookcase (`dogfood/shelf.aix`)

Files: `dogfood/shelf.aix` (the program), `dogfood/shelf_lib.aix` (the library), probes `dogfood/shelf_probe_band.aix`, `shelf_probe_speed.aix`, `shelf_probe_overlap.aix`, `shelf_probe_hang.aix`, `shelf_probe_hang2.aix`, `shelf_probe_ground.aix`, `shelf_probe_fail.aix`. 11 renders (2 quick sheets, a quick beauty of a probe, and 8 full: two with `--beauty`, two `--beauty-only`, one `--focus`, one moving the slice, one of the library's plate, and the first), about 64 checks (40 of them bisecting two hangs in `overlap()`), and 10 other commands (`doc` once, `fmt --check` eight times, `fmt` once to see its diff).

## 1. The finished bookcase

An oak bookcase 8 wide, 9.95 tall and 3 deep, one unit being 10 cm (so 80 × 99.5 × 30 cm), front to +z. The case is `lib.case(case, spacing)` with `case = {w: 8, d: 3, t: 0.2, plinth: 0.6, back: 0.1}` and `spacing = {count: 3, gap: 2.85}`: two side panels (grain up), a back panel 0.1 thick, a kick board under the bottom shelf set 0.1 back from the front, and four boards (grain along x), the bottom board, two shelves and the top, each set 0.04 into the sides and the back. Its height is derived from the spacing (`case_height = plinth + (count + 1) t + count gap`), and `shelf_top(c, s, i)` gives the y of shelf i's top face (0.8, 3.85, 6.9).

On it stand 33 books, every one a record `{w, h, d, color, tilt}` in a list per shelf, placed by one loop that carries a cursor along x:

- bottom shelf: a marble bookend at the left, nine uprights, and a book 2.5 tall leaning 18 degrees to the left onto the last upright;
- middle shelf: a stack of four lying flat at the left (2.4, 2.2, 2.05 and 1.8 long, 1.5 high in all), eight uprights, and a book leaning 15 degrees to the right onto the right side panel;
- top shelf: a book leaning 20 degrees to the left onto the left side panel, six uprights, a marble bookend holding them on the right, and a stack of three lying flat at the right.

A book (`lib.book`) is a box with `round=0.02` in its colour, a cream decal on its top, bottom and fore-edge inset 0.03 from each cover (the pages), and two gold bands decalled across the spine. Its origin is the edge it stands on (bottom-left for an upright or a left lean, bottom-right for a right lean; flat books lie on their left cover), it is turned about that edge by `tilt` and then `ground()`ed. A bookend (`lib.bookend`) is a quarter-ellipse 0.7 long and 1.7 tall extruded 1.5 deep, flat face on x = 0. The leaning books are placed by the geometry of the lean: a left lean's standing edge is `min(h sin t, H tan t)` right of its neighbour (its top corner on the neighbour's face, or its face on the neighbour's top corner, whichever comes first), a right lean's top corner `h sin t` short of the panel.

The promises, all in the program and all holding (`asserts: 271 pass`): for each of the 33 books, (1) not sunk into its shelf, `clearance(bk, board) > -0.002`, lowest reading `-3.608e-16`; (2) not above its shelf or the book under it, `clearance(bk, board) - F * stack_y < 0.005`, highest `0.002779` (book 9, the leaning one; see friction 7 for why it is not 0); (3) not into the book or panel before it, `clearance(bk, prev) > -0.002`, lowest `-8.153e-16` (stacked books, resting exactly); (4) under the shelf above with room, `clearance(bk, above) > 0.05`, lowest `0.23`; (5) a stacked book rests on the one under it, `< 0.005`, all `≤ 0`; (6) no book passes through any book before it, `overlap(bk, books) < 0.0001`, all `0`; (7) a left-leaning book leans on what is before it, `clearance < 0.012`, `0.009574` and `0.007728`; (8) the right-leaning book leans on the panel, `0.0065`. And for the whole: 33 books (`n >= 20`), the case is one piece, the model's lowest point is on y = 0, both bookends stand on their shelves (`2.776e-17`, `2.498e-16`), the right bookend clears the top row by `0.06`, the slice probe touches its shelf (`0.002779`). The report: `Watertight | yes`, `Pieces | 1`, `Stands | yes, centre of mass 1.35 inside the footprint`.

Against the plan: it is the plan, with three changes. I added a second stack on the top shelf after the first quick sheet showed that shelf empty to the right of its bookend (33 books instead of 30). The gap a leaning book leaves to what it leans on went from 0.06 (as between uprights) to 0.002 at the square corner, after the focus close-up showed a leaning book standing visibly off its neighbour, leaning on nothing; it is now about 0.007 off (0.7 mm), which `overlap()` survives (friction 16). And "the case stands" is not a promise in the program, because nothing can promise it (friction 13); it is the report's Stands row. Departures I accepted: the books stand exactly on the boards, so the mesher fuses them with the case into one watertight piece (true to "not in it", and no seam appeared); bookends hold their rows a 0.06 gap off, not pressed; the gold bands read as zigzags on the sheet (a 0.06 band at a 0.05 cell) and as clean bands in the beauty render.

## 3. Friction

1. **No conditional anywhere in the docs.** Planning the loop (a book is upright, leaning left, leaning right or lying in a stack, and each is placed differently), I searched `docs/language.md`, `docs/reference.md`, `docs/agent-guide.md` and the skill for `if`, "conditional", "ternary", "while": nothing. The docs never say outright that there is none; I inferred it. Comparisons are numbers (1 or 0), so the plan is to select numbers with flag arithmetic (`x = U * a + L * b + ...`). A shape cannot be selected that way, so a part that exists only for some records (a bookend after book k) cannot be in the loop at all. Cost: a design decision up front (bookends outside the loop, every per-kind number written as a sum of flag products). Expected: one line saying "there is no `if`; select a number with a comparison, `(t > 0) * a`".
2. **A `def` body is one expression, so a library part has no local names and no loops.** The book's cover, page block and spine bands need `b.w`, `b.h` and `b.d` in a dozen places and cannot name an intermediate; a case's shelves cannot be a `for` inside its `def`. The language doc does say "A `def` body is one expression", but nothing says how to write a part that needs a loop (answer I chose: `array(count + 1, 0, step, 0)` for the boards, helper `def`s for each sub-part). Cost: the library is split into many tiny defs.
3. **A false "paints nothing" warning on a decal whose region is a union.** `check dogfood/shelf_lib.aix` warned three times, once per book on the plate: ``warning: line 74: decal(): the region `band_region(b)` touches none of the shape's surface, so it paints nothing; the region must cross the surface (a sphere centred on the skin, a box through it)``. The region is two boxes 0.06 tall and 0.2 deep, centred on the spine face at `z = d/2`, so each one plainly crosses the surface. Probe `dogfood/shelf_probe_band.aix`: a single box of the same size gives no warning (at a 0.018 cell and at a 0.053 cell), but the same two boxes joined with `+`, written inline or returned by a `def`, are warned at a 0.05 cell. `--quick --beauty` on the probe (`out/probe_band/beauty.png`) shows both gold bands painted on every book. So the warning is wrong, and it is wrong in the confident form ("paints nothing"), which would have sent me to rebuild a region that worked. Cost: one probe file, one check, one quick render. Expected: no warning, or one that says the test is unsure for a region thinner than a cell. I kept the bands and ignore that warning from here on.
4. **A thin-part note names the plate, not the part.** `note: Parts between 1.2 and 2 cells thick mesh but often not watertight: 'plate' (1.9 cells); thicken them to two cells or raise the grid.` The thin part is the 0.1 back panel, built inside `case()` and called inline in `plate`. The doc does say that a def called inline has no name of its own, but the note could still say what the thin thing is (a box 0.1 deep, at z = -1.45). Cost: arithmetic to work out which member is 1.9 cells (0.1 / 0.0539).
5. **`check` never finished: `overlap()` hangs on a book resting on a board.** The first `npx tsx src/cli.ts check dogfood/shelf.aix` (30 books, six asserts each inside the loop) printed nothing for 12 minutes at 100% of a CPU, and I killed it (exit 144). Without the asserts the same program checks in 0.76 s (`dogfood/shelf_probe_speed.aix`). Timing one assert kind at a time: `clearance(bk, board)`, `clearance(bk, prev)` and `overlap(bk, books)` each cost nothing; `overlap(bk, board)` alone over the bottom row ran past a 100 s timeout. Bisected over eleven more checks to `dogfood/shelf_probe_hang.aix`: `board = box(7.68, 0.2, 2.94) | move(0, 0.8 - 0.2 / 2, 0.03)`, `bk = box(0.35, 2.55, 1.7) | move(0, 2.55 / 2, 0) | ground() | move(-2, 0.8, 0.3)`, `assert overlap(bk, board) < 0.0001` does not return within 15 s, where without the `ground()`, or with a box 2.2 tall instead of 2.55, or with the board's height written as a literal 0.7, it returns `0` in 0.4 s. So a face that meets another to within floating point (the board's top is 0.8000000000000002, the book's bottom 0.8 less whatever `ground()` leaves) sends `overlap()` into a loop with no output, no progress and no time limit. The docs promise "zero when they only touch" and that an overlap too thin for the lattice is reported as such; neither happened. Cost: about 25 minutes and 16 runs of `check`. What I did: replaced "not sunk into the shelf" with `clearance(bk, board) > -0.002`, which says the same thing and is instant. Expected: `overlap` to return 0, or `check` to print which assert it is working on so a hang can at least be found without bisecting.
6. **`check` prints an assert with its parentheses dropped, so it reads as a different expression.** Source line 103: `assert F * (stack_y > 0) * clearance(bk, prev) < 0.005, ...`. Printed: `assert (line 103) holds: F * stack_y > 0 * clearance(bk, prev) < 0.005 is 0 < 0.005: book 9 rests on the book under it`. With comparisons the loosest operators, the printed text parses as `(F * stack_y) > (0 * clearance(...)) < 0.005`, which is not what was tested. Expected: the expression as written, or at least with the parentheses its precedence needs. Cost: a moment's alarm that my promise was parsed wrongly, and a look at the source to be sure it was not.
7. **`clearance()` reads a few thousandths for a leaning book that touches its shelf.** The leaning books passed "not above its shelf" at `0.002779`, `0.0003708` and `0.001046`, though `ground()` set each one's lowest point on the board. Probe `dogfood/shelf_probe_ground.aix`: a box 0.34 × 2.5 × 1.9 with `round=0.02`, turned 18 degrees about its bottom edge, then `ground()`ed (its box drops by exactly 0.0052, the rounded edge's lift, 0.02 × (sin 18 + cos 18 - 1)), reads `clearance(round_g, floor) < 0.0001 is 0.00373 < 0.0001, closest at (2, 0, -0.75)`; the same box set down by that number instead of by `ground()`, at another x, reads `0.0006816`; a sharp box turned the same way reads `0` placed by the numbers and `-0.00004426` after `ground()`. So the reading depends on where the part sits, and on a rounded edge it is several thousandths off. The language doc says clearance "is exact for parts that face each other" and "can miss a feature narrower than a twelfth of the part"; a rounded edge resting on a plane is that narrow feature, but nothing says it is. Cost: one probe, one check. What I did: the resting promise is `clearance(bk, board) < 0.005`, half a millimetre at this scale, and the report says so.
8. **`--slice` through a leaning part puts the vertical plane where the part is in the air.** `render dogfood/shelf.aix --slice lean_probe`: the `X = 1.23` panel of `slices.png` cuts the leaning book at its centroid, which on a book leaning 18 degrees is a slanted band 1.2 to 2.2 above the shelf, so that panel shows a maroon rectangle floating between two shelves: the opposite of what the cut was for. The `Z = 0.4` panel is the proof (the book's bottom-left edge on the board, its top against its neighbour). The language doc's account of `--slice` ("through the step's solid centroid, or through its widest section ... never the box's centre, which a leaning canopy ... puts in the air") does not cover a part whose centroid plane misses its contact. Expected: a way to say "cut where it touches", or a word that a contact is cut with `set slice_x` at the contact. Cost: one full render (24 s) read, then a second render with `slice_x` moved to the contact edge.
9. **A loop's working names become steps, thumbnails and callouts.** Every name assigned inside the loop (`bk`, `prev`, `board`, `above`) is a step holding its last iteration's value. `steps.png` has thumbnails `7. PREV` and `8. BK` (both the last flat book of the top stack) and `5. BOARD` (the top row's board only). `callouts.png` labels that one flat book `BK`, as if `bk` were a part of the model, and the report's callout line reads `bookcase, books, bookend_r, bk, bookend_l`. The 33 books themselves cannot be named, focused, sliced or blamed one by one: the language has no name built from a loop index (the doc's way, `gondola_1 = car(1, "gondola_1")`, is one line per copy written out). Cost: a probe step (`lean_probe`) that rebuilds one book from the loop's recorded position, so that `--slice` has a name to go through. Expected: a way to name a step per iteration, or the loop's own names kept off the sheets.
10. **The decal false warning swamps the sheet and the report.** The sheet's title bar says `32 WARNINGS IN REPORT.MD`, and every one is the false `band_region` warning of point 3, once per book. A real warning would be invisible among them, so I read the report through `grep -v band_region` from here on. The report's Materials row also lists `#efe6cf` (the page paper) 35 times, once per decal, in a row of 70 entries.
11. **`fmt --check` names the file but not the line, and its fix breaks a column of comments.** `npx tsx src/cli.ts fmt --check dogfood/shelf_lib.aix` passed (exit 0, no output). `npx tsx src/cli.ts fmt --check dogfood/shelf.aix` said `dogfood/shelf.aix: not formatted; aixle fmt --write dogfood/shelf.aix` (exit 1), with no line number and no hint what was wrong. Diffing `aixle fmt dogfood/shelf.aix` against the file found one change: line 83, `prev_h = spacing.gap # how tall ...` became `prev_h = spacing.gap  # how tall ...`. That comment sat in a column of five trailing comments, all at column 23; my line's code simply ended one space before the column. `fmt` moved that one comment to column 24 and left the other four at 23, so its own layout breaks the column, though `docs/language.md` says a trailing comment "keeps its column unless the code now reaches it, so a column of them stays a column" and that a column is "pushed right together when one line's code now reaches past it". The rule that seems to be at work (two spaces before a trailing comment) is not written anywhere. Cost: a diff by hand, and moving all five comments to column 24 myself, after which `fmt --check` passed. Expected: `--check` to print the line and the change (a one-line diff), and the column moved together.
12. **`aixle doc` lists constants by name only, and the plate's helpers with them.** Its last line: `Constants: case_default, spacing_default, book_default, bookend_default, sink, side_wood, board_wood, page_paper, demo_case, demo_spacing, demo_y`. A caller wants the values (`case_default = {w: 8, d: 3, t: 0.2, plinth: 0.6, back: 0.1}` is the documentation of what a case record holds) and has no use for `demo_*`, which only build the library's own `show` plate. There is no way to keep a library name private. The parameters show as `case(c, s)` with nothing to say `c` is a record or which fields it reads; I wrote the fields in the header comment, and `doc` printed that header in full, which worked. Cost: none in time; a reader of `doc` gets a weaker page.
13. **"The case stands" cannot be promised.** The brief's third promise is the one the report already judges (`| Stands | yes, centre of mass 1.35 inside the footprint |`), but the reference has no query for it: no `stands()`, no centre of mass, no footprint. `pieces`, `overhang`, `wall`, `void`, `overlap`, `inside` and `clearance` are there; the Physics row is not. So the promise lives in the report, where the next edit can silently break it, which is what the docs say asserts are for. What I did: `assert abs(bottom(model)) < 0.001` (on the floor) and `assert pieces(bookcase) == 1` as stand-ins, and quote the report's Stands row. Cost: a promise I could not write.
14. **`check --brief` still prints every passing assert; a loop's promises are 200 lines.** With six promises inside a 33-book loop plus five outside, `check --brief` printed 203 `assert (line N) holds: ...` lines. The four deliberate failures (see section 2) were in among them, in line order ("failures among the passes"), so the only way to read the result was `grep fails`. A flag for failures only, or one line per assert line in a loop ("line 106: 33 of 33 hold, closest 0.0028 at book 9"), would have made the output readable. Cost: grepping every check from here on.
15. **What a negative `clearance()` measures is not what "how deep they overlap" suggests.** In the failure probe a book sunk 0.03 into its shelf read `-0.03`, a book pushed so it overlaps its neighbour by 0.04 read `-0.04`, but a book 3.0 tall under a 2.85 gap, whose top is 0.15 into the board above, read `clearance(bk, above) > 0.05 is -0.05845 > 0.05`. The reference says "negative by how deep they overlap"; 0.058 is neither the 0.15 the book reaches into the board nor half of it. The failure still fired and its place, `(-1.89, 9.89, -0.64)`, was right. Cost: a minute's arithmetic; a promise with a threshold on the depth would be unreliable.
16. **`overlap()` hangs again, on a leaning book.** Making the leaning books touch what they lean on (`lean_gap = 0`, so a book's square top corner sits on its neighbour's face and its rounding leaves 0.005) sent `check dogfood/shelf.aix --brief` past a 300 s timeout: `Terminated`, nothing printed. Removing the one assert `overlap(bk, books) < 0.0001` brought it back to 3.2 s; running the loop over one row at a time hung on the bottom row only (the row whose last book leans on a book, not a panel). Minimal: `dogfood/shelf_probe_hang2.aix`, two plain boxes, `lean = box(0.34, 2.5, 1.9) | move(0.17, 1.25, 0) | rotate(z=18) | move(0.30 + 2.5 * sin(18), 0, 0)` against `prev = box(0.30, 2.45, 1.9) | move(0.15, 1.225, 0)`: `overlap(lean, prev)` does not return in 20 s; with both boxes `round=0.02` (0.005 apart) it does not return either; with the lean moved 0.005 further right and no rounding it returns `0` in 0.46 s. So this is not only the floating-point face of point 5: a corner on (or a few thousandths off) a face hangs `overlap()` too, and a leaning book is that case by definition. Cost: one 300 s timeout, five more checks to find it. What I did: kept `overlap(bk, books)` (the gross "no book passes through another" check, which is fine at the 0.06 gaps) and set `lean_gap = 0.002`, so the rounded corner stands about 0.007 off its neighbour (0.7 mm at this scale); with that it returns, and the promise that a leaning book leans on something is `clearance < 0.012`. Expected: an `overlap()` that terminates, whatever the geometry.
17. **Smaller things that cost a read each.** (a) The quick sheet's warning `quick pass: 1 step is thinner than this pass's 0.155 cell and may be missing or broken on this sheet (model)` names the output, `model`, not the member (the 0.1 back panel, built inside `lib.case()`). (b) A bookcase that is not a print gets a full Print section: `Summary | one piece, watertight; a wall 0.1 mm thin, support under 12% of the surface; units assumed to be millimetres` and `85% of the surface stands on walls under 0.8 mm`; there is nothing to act on there and it is the longest section of the report. (c) `check --brief` opens with a line listing all 24 names the library exports (`use lib shelf_lib: case_default, spacing_default, ...`). (d) `show` must come after the steps it names: my probe with `show board + up + lean + right + flat` above the lines defining them failed with `line 8: 'lean' is not defined`, which is fair, but the language doc's "the file ends by showing one shape" reads as if `show` could go anywhere.

## 4. What worked well

- **Records as parameters.** `{...book_default, ...b}` for defaults, a list of records per shelf, a field holding a shape (`first: bookend_l`) and a material (`mat: material("marble", scale=0.4)`), `rows[0].books[9]` and `placed[9][0]`: all as the language doc says, first time. The program reads as a table of books.
- **A loop with real state.** A cursor, a running stack height, the previous book's shape and height, lists grown with `+ [ ... ]`, a nested loop to find the stack's width: all of it worked, and asserts inside a loop body work and run per iteration (the docs never say so, but nothing stopped me).
- **The failure messages.** Every deliberate mistake was caught by the promise written for it, with the measured number, the place, and my own message naming the book. That is the tool at its best: four misplaced books out of 33 found in 3.8 s without a picture.
- **`clearance()` for resting.** Apart from its few-thousandths error on a rounded edge, it is fast, signed, and its failures say where; it replaced `overlap()` wherever `overlap()` hung.
- **`ground()` after a turn** dropped each leaning book by exactly the lift its rounded edge gives (0.0052 at 18 degrees, matching `0.02 × (sin 18 + cos 18 - 1)`).
- **Touching is fine here.** The docs warn that parts that only touch leave a seam or a second piece. Thirty-three books standing exactly on their boards, and stacked books exactly on each other, meshed watertight and in one piece at grid 200; I did not have to sink anything, which kept the "not in it" promise honest.
- **`--slice` on a region and `set slice_x` with an expression** put the proof picture exactly at the leaning book's standing edge; a camera with `focus=` on the same region gave the beauty close-up.
- **Speed.** Evaluation 0.8 s, 271 promises in under 4 s, a full render at grid 200 with beauty in 40 s, a quick sheet in 4 s.
- **`aixle doc`** printed my header comment block whole, which is where a record-taking library has to document its records.

## 5. Three changes that would have helped most

1. **Make `overlap()` terminate, and let `check` show what it is working on.** Two different near-contacts (a face meeting a face to within floating point after `ground()`, and a turned corner on a face) made `overlap()` spin forever, and `check` printed nothing, so a 30-book program looked hung for twelve minutes and took about forty checks to bisect. A leaning book against a neighbour and a book on a shelf are exactly the cases `overlap()` is advertised for. A time or sample limit with an honest answer ("they touch"), and a line per assert as it runs (or on a slow one), would have saved most of this round's time.
