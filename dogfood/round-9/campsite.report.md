# Round 9 dogfood: campsite at night (`dogfood/campsite.aix`)

Worktree: `/home/user/aixle/.claude/worktrees/round9-campsite`. Nothing committed; scratch copies lived in the session scratchpad and their `out/` folders were deleted.

## 1. The finished scene

A `scene` of seven objects on a 6 × 6 × 0.12 dirt slab whose top is y = 0 (everything else stands on 0). At the origin a campfire: four leaning wood logs, a two-tone flame (an outer orange `glow=1.0` blend of three cones with a pale-yellow `glow=1.6` core cone), a glowing ember disc; round it eleven varied stones on a 0.72 ring built in a `for` loop with `rand()`. Back-left a ridge tent: a triangle profile `extrude(...,"z")`, `shell(0.06)`, a triangular door cut through the front wall, turned 35° so the door faces the fire; the slices confirm a real void with the door gap in the Y cut. Front-right a 2-unit walnut pole in a stump, a short arm, an iron hook, iron caps, a `glass` chimney (`shell(0.05)` of a named `shade_space` cylinder) with a glowing mantle cylinder hanging in the air inside. A split-log oak bench on two log feet at z = 1.45, and a 3-2-1 firewood stack back-right. Six asserts (two `void`, two `inside`, one `overlap`, one `clearance`) all pass; every object row in the report says Stands: yes. Two lights (a cool high "moon", a warm "fire" from above-front), three cameras (`wide`, `lantern` focused on `lantern_head`, `fireside` focused on `stones`), `set environment night`, `set ambient 1.6`. Geometry came out as planned first time; the presentation took four rounds (dark first render, a camera that framed the wrong thing and was occluded by the pole, a lantern whose glow refracted into rings, a fireside shot with the fire tiny behind the bench).

## 2. Verification

**Asserts** (all on `check`, ~2 s each):
- `void(tent_void, tent) == 1`: passed first time, printed `is 1 == 1`.
- `void(tent_void, others) == 1` (others = campfire + stones + lantern + bench + firewood): passed `1 == 1`. Deliberately broken by moving the firewood into the tent: `assert (line 90) fails: void(tent_void, others) == 1 is 0 == 1: nothing else pokes into the tent`, exit code 1. A bare 0: no hint which part or where.
- `inside(flame, hearth) == 1` (hearth = a named cylinder of the ring's inner air): `1 == 1` first time.
- `inside(mantle, shade_space) == 1`: `1 == 1`. Broken by sliding the mantle 0.2 sideways: `fails: ... is 0 == 1`. Also measured: `inside(mantle, shade) == 1` against the *hollow* shade is `0 == 1` (the mantle is in the air, not in the glass), and `inside(mantle, lantern_head) == 1` is trivially 1 because the union contains the mantle.
- `overlap(shade, pole) < 0.0001`: `0 < 0.0001`.
- `clearance(bench, campfire) > 0.4`: `0.896 > 0.4`. Broken by moving the bench to z = 0.85: `fails: clearance(bench, campfire) > 0.4 is 0.2961 > 0.4` (the number is the useful kind of failure).
- Per-object Stands rows: first full render `lantern | no, 0.218 outside` (a 2-unit pole with a lantern hung 0.45 off one side on an 0.08 footprint); fixed with a 0.6-wide stump base → `yes, 0.173 inside`. All others yes from the start.

**Environments**: `night` first render was almost black except the flame (moon power 0.45 invisible, ground nearly black). With moon power 0.9 and `set ambient 1.6` it reads as a night scene. `sunset` (quick beauty on a copy): the whole picture one brown wash, sky a flat pink-brown, the flame and lantern lose against it. Kept `night`.

**Shots**:

- `lantern`: wrong twice. `focus="lantern"` framed the whole 2-tall object so the head was a speck at the top, and at azimuth 60 the pole stood in front of the head. Restructured so `lantern_head` is a placed named step, moved the camera to azimuth -110, zoom 0.8 (zoom-out on a focus shot works; undocumented). Then the glass read as a dark jar with white refraction rings/bars over three renders (sphere mantle r 0.05, 0.06, 0.075, cylinder r 0.05, all within a cell of the inner wall). - `fireside` (az -25, el 5, zoom 1.5): fire tiny behind the bench, scene small in frame. `focus="stones"`, el 12: bench foreground, flame and logs above it, tent left, firewood behind. Good.
- Lights: "moon" as a cool low-power key from elevation 65 is not distinguishable from the ambient in any shot. "Fire" tried at azimuth -10 elevation 8 (lit the bench's camera side and the tent's door face like a sunset from the front) and at azimuth 20 elevation 35 (reads as a warm cast on tops and the tent door; kept). Neither lights the fire-facing sides of all the objects, because a light is a direction. Glow materials do not light neighbours: the stones' inner faces and the ground under the flame stay dark.

## 3. Friction, one per bullet

- **Assert-only regions are warned as errors.** Lines `tent_void = place_tent(extrude(polygon(-0.7, 0.08, 0.7, 0.08, 0, 0.95), tent_d - 0.25, "z"))`, `hearth = cylinder(ring_r - 0.16, 1.2) | move(0, 0.6, 0)`, `others = campfire + stones + lantern + bench + firewood`, and originally five more (`shade_placed`, `shade_space`, `mantle_placed`, `pole_placed`, `tent_inside`). Expected: a step used in an `assert` counts as used, as a decal region does. Got: `warning: 'hearth' (line 48) is not part of the output; add it to the model or remove it` — eight of them, both fixes wrong. Worked around by deriving geometry from the region (`shade = shade_space | shell(0.05)`) and a `def place_pole(s)` helper; three remain, and `steps.png` frames them UNUSED (tent_void grey, hearth and others red, for no reason I could see).
- **`inside(a, b)` needs the solid, not the enclosure.** The brief's "mantle inside its shade" written as `inside(mantle, shade)` is `0` (measured) because the shade is a shell. I had to keep the un-hollowed `shade_space` alive as a named step. The doc's `assert inside(spring, box) == 1` does not say the housing must be the filled volume; a sentence, and ideally a warning when `b` is a shell.
- **A grouped part cannot be focused on after "build, rotate, move".** `camera("lantern", focus="lantern", ...)` framed the whole object; `focus="shade"` would have framed the pre-placement box at the origin, since `shade` was named before `| rotate(y=-30) | move(2.1, 0, 0.9)`. The doc lists this only for array/ring/mirror. Workaround: `def place_pole(s)` applied to every part so each placed step has a name, and a `lantern_head` union. The skill's own recipe (build at origin, then move the union) produces steps you cannot focus or assert on.
- **A focus shot can be occluded without a word.** `camera("lantern", focus="lantern_head", azimuth=60, elevation=12)`: the pole stood between camera and head; the report said the shot was "on lantern_head". Cost one full render. A note like "focus is 70% hidden by 'pole'" would have saved it.
- **Night is very dark by default and the doc does not say to lift ambient.** First `beauty.png` under `set environment night` with `light("moon", ..., power=0.45)` was black ground, black stones, a flame. Needed `set ambient 1.6` and moon 0.9. The language doc says night is where "a glow material carries the picture" but not that the ambient is scaled down or by how much.
- **Lights are directions; a fire cannot be a light.** `light("fire", azimuth=-10, elevation=8, ...)` lit the camera-facing sides (a sunset look); `azimuth=20, elevation=35` reads as warm ambient. No point light and no `position=`; the doc's "several lights" section does not warn that a low-elevation light means "from the horizon", not "from that thing".
- **Glow does not illuminate.** `material("#ff7a18", glow=1.0)` on the flame: the stones' inner faces and the ground stay dark. The doc's "gives off its own light in every render" reads as if it did. One sentence: glow is self-illumination only.
- **A glowing part within a cell of a glass wall renders as rings and bars.** `mantle = place_pole(sphere(0.075) | ...)` inside `shade_space = cylinder(0.11, 0.3)` shelled 0.05 (inner r 0.06): three full renders of a dark jar with white refraction bands, plus `7 edges ... in 'shade', 'mantle'` in the watertight row. Fix: `cylinder(0.03, 0.2, round=0.015)`, a cell clear. The language doc's lantern advice (hollow, lamp in the air) is right but stops short of the clearance rule inside glass.
- **`--quick --beauty` does not judge glass.** Quick beauty (320 px) showed the shade as a pale frosted opaque cylinder with no mantle; the full render showed dark glass with the mantle. Two variants (`material("glass", glow=0.5)` vs `paint("amber")`) were pixel-identical in quick and clearly different at full size. The guide's "a small beauty render in a few seconds to try materials" does not hold for transmit materials.
- **`glow` on a transmit material does nothing visible.** `material("glass", glow=0.5)` at full size looked the same as `glass`; the report lists it as `glass*`. Either unsupported or too weak; the reference's `glow` line does not say.
- **Void/inside failures carry no location.** `void(tent_void, others) == 1 is 0 == 1` and `inside(mantle, shade_space) == 1 is 0 == 1`; `clearance` prints the number and is the model. `void` could name the first solid sample's point and the innermost step there.
- **Callouts count and the report disagree.** Title "20 of 23", report names one unlabelled step (`seat`); first render "13 of 15" with no unlabelled list at all; no `callouts` key in `report.json` to read the rest. The title text "THE LARGEST? THE REST IN REPORT.MD" reads like a stray question mark.
- **Callouts label ancestors and coincident steps twice.** Both `firewood` and `firewood_stack`, `bench` and `seat_log`, `tent` and `door`, `shade` and `shade_space` (a region whose surface *is* the shade's outer surface), and `flame_outer` rather than `flame` or `campfire`; `mantle` is labelled through the glass. The bottom column of six labels has crossing leaders and a label at bottom-centre for a part at the right edge; two leaders I could not follow to their end.
- **Watertight "alone" hid the real cause on the firewood.** `firewood_stack = wood_log(0, 0.09, -0.2) + wood_log(0, 0.09, 0) + ...` (six equal cylinders touching along lines) gave 86 edges "in 'firewood_stack' alone (no other part within a cell: a crease of its own surface, or the mesher's noise)". The cause was tangent logs of the same step; the guide's sink-by-a-cell rule fixed it (spacing 0.22, rows at 0.19/0.29). "alone" should say "two placements of the same step touch".
- **`check` and the report disagree on thin walls.** `shade = shade_space | shell(0.05)` at cell 0.03: `check` is silent (its threshold is 1.2 cells), the report says "Parts between 1.2 and 2 cells thick mesh but often not watertight: 'shade' (1.7 cells); thicken them to two cells". Earlier at 0.035 `check` said "Thicken it to 0.06 (two cells) or raise the grid (set grid 429)". One threshold, or `check` should print the report's note.
- **Zoom on a scene never gets closer than the slab's corners.** `camera("wide", ..., zoom=1.1)` and `zoom=1.3` gave the same framing with the scene in the bottom half of the frame; `fireside` at `zoom=1.5` the same. Only `focus=` frames tighter, which then names a step rather than a composition. A `look_at`/`frame=[a, b, c]` of several steps would have made the fireside shot without the stones as a proxy.
- **The moon light cannot be seen doing anything.** `light("moon", azimuth=140, elevation=65, size=2.5, color="#9fb4dc", power=0.9)`: no cool cast I could point at in any shot, and no way to render one light alone or read its contribution. I do not know whether it works.
- **Loop variables print as steps.** `a = 327`, `s = 1.02` in `check` output and 34 "named shapes" on steps.png for a 7-object scene; harmless noise.
- **Ground-slab note.** `note: the lowest point of the surface is at y = -0.12; pipe the model through ground()` on every run for a slab that is deliberately below 0; unavoidable with a ground whose top is 0, and the guide's scene section suggests exactly that layout.
- **Time.** A full render at grid 200 is ~30 s (mesh 2.3 s, exports 3 s, steps mesh 2.2 s, three beauty shots 2.3–4.1 s each); `--no-export --no-viewer` trims it. Acceptable, but every camera experiment costs a full render because quick does not show glass and there is no flag to render one camera.

## 4. What worked well

- `check` in 2 s with every step's box, the `surface` lines for turned parts, and every assert printed with its numbers; the failure format for `clearance` is exactly right.
- The assert vocabulary itself: `void`, `inside`, `overlap`, `clearance` covered every promise in the brief once I knew to name the solid volumes; all six passed on first evaluation with the geometry as planned.
- The report's per-object Assembly rows caught the tipping lantern with a clear number (`no, 0.218 outside`) and the fix was obvious.
- Slices through the tent (first object in `scene`) showed the void and the door gap without any extra work.
- `camera(focus=)` with `zoom=0.8` and `focus="stones"` as a framing proxy made two of the three shots; `beauty_<name>.png` naming and the report's Cameras/Lights/Environment lines match the program exactly.
- `def place_x(s)` helpers, `for` with `rand()`, `union(k=)` for the flame, two-tone glow: the language did everything asked without a single syntax or type error across the whole session.
- Thin-part warnings from `check` (hook 0.02, shade 0.035) with the exact thickness to use.

## 5. Three changes that would have helped most

1. **Treat a step used only in `assert` (or `decal`) as used**: no "not part of the output" warning, no UNUSED frame; and make `void`/`inside` failures say where (the first offending sample point and the innermost step there).
2. **A section in the language doc on presenting a night scene**: that `night` scales ambient down and what `set ambient` to start from; that lights are directions and a fire's warmth is best faked from above; that `glow` illuminates nothing; that a glowing part must clear a glass wall by a cell; that `--quick --beauty` does not show transmit materials; whether `glow` works on glass. Plus a warning when a camera's focus step is mostly hidden by another step.
3. **Callouts that tell the truth about what they show**: a count that matches the report's unlabelled list, a `callouts` entry in `report.json`, one label per visible surface (not `shade` and `shade_space`, `firewood` and `firewood_stack`), and leaders laid out so a label sits near its part rather than in a column at the bottom.
