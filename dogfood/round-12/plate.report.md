# Round 12: motor mounting plate (`dogfood/plate.aix`)

Files: `dogfood/plate.aix` (the program), three deliberate-failure probes `dogfood/plate_probe_fail_through.aix`, `dogfood/plate_probe_fail_blind.aix`, `dogfood/plate_probe_fail_pocket.aix`, and this report; renders in `out/plate/` (final), `out/plate_tq/` (three-quarter lighting loop) and `out/plate_probe_fail_through/`. **15 renders** (1 `--quick`, 4 full, 10 `--beauty-only`), **10 checks**, and other commands: 1 `doc` lookup, 1 `fmt --check`, about 14 Python crops and pixel reads of the PNGs, the worktree setup.

## 1. The finished plate

A machined aluminium mounting plate for a NEMA 17 stepper motor, in millimetres (`set units mm`), lying flat on y = 0 with its front at +z: **120 × 12 × 80**, its corners rounded R5 in plan, at `set grid 256` (cell 0.469). Its features, every one a cut from the blank:

- **Four counterbored through holes** at (±50, ±30) for the plate's own M5 screws: 5.5 straight through, a 10 counterbore 5.5 deep.
- **The motor bore**, 22.5 straight through, at (-20, 0), for the motor's 22 mm shaft boss.
- **Four blind tapped-size holes** (M3 tap drill, 2.5) on a 31 square round the bore: 8 deep to the full diameter, then a 118-degree drill point (0.75 more), so each stops 3.25 above the far face.
- **A pocket** 34 × 46 with R4 corners (an 8 mm end mill), milled 5 deep into the top at (25, -3): its floor is at y = 7, so it takes 5 of the plate's 12.
- **The part number** `MP17-001`, cap height 5, stroke 1.1, `spacing=1`, engraved 0.6 deep along the front.

Volume of the plate 99,845 mm³ (about 270 g of aluminium at 2.70 g/cm³; the report's "133.8 g of PLA" is the scene's, see F2/F12). Watertight, one piece.

**Materials.** `aluminium = material("#d9dcdf", metal=1, rough=0.6)`: a metal of my own, there being no preset (F1), finished bead-blasted rather than polished (section 4). The **backlight** under it, `material("#8cc4ff", glow=1.6)`, a glowing panel 112 × 1 × 72 inset 4 under the plate's edge so no shot sees it except through a hole.

**Lights and cameras.** `light("key", azimuth=-135, elevation=55, size=2)` (the plan camera's azimuth less 135, per the docs' plan-shot rule) and `light("fill", azimuth=40, elevation=30, power=0.35)`, in the default studio. `camera("plan", azimuth=0, elevation=90, zoom=1.1)` is `set camera` and so `beauty.png`; `camera("three_quarter", azimuth=-35, elevation=45, zoom=1.2)` is `beauty_three_quarter.png`. `set slice section_tapped` puts the slices through the row of tapped holes at z = 15.5. Output: `scene plate, backlight`.

**Promises: 33 asserts, all holding** (final `check`):

| --- | --- | --- |
| one piece | `pieces(plate) == 1` | 1 |
| each corner hole goes through | `void(cylinder(2, 20) at the hole, plate)`, one rod per hole, 4 times | holds 4 times |

**Against the plan.** The plate is as planned in every dimension. Departures: (1) the backlight panel, which was not in the plan: the plan shot could not tell a through hole from a blind one under any light (section 4), so the plate rests on a glowing panel 1 mm thick and the model is a two-object scene whose lowest point is y = -1; (2) the finish went from a polished machined `rough=0.35` to a bead-blasted `0.6`; (3) the letters needed `spacing=1` (F3); (4) the tapped holes are tap-drill size with no thread; no edge breaks or chamfers; (5) two slicing slabs (`section_tapped`, `section_corner`) exist only to aim the slices, and `section_corner` leaves the run's one warning (F5); (6) the three-quarter camera went from 35 to 45 degrees up to see into the counterbores.

## 3. Friction

**F1. No aluminium preset.** The brief is a machined aluminium plate, the commonest machined metal there is. The preset table (`docs/reference.md`, Material presets) has `steel`, `silver`, `chrome`, `iron`, `gold`, `brass`, `copper`, `bronze`, and language.md's metal list explains each F0, but nothing for aluminium. Expected an `aluminium` (or `aluminum`) preset or at least its F0 in the metals paragraph. I guessed `material("#d9dcdf", metal=1, rough=0.35)` from memory of aluminium's reflectance (~0.91 linear); cost: no render, but a guess where the other metals are measured.

**F2. `set units mm` makes a machined part a print.** The program says `set units mm` so the sheet and `check` speak millimetres; the report then has a Print section: "Summary | one piece, watertight; a wall 0.234 mm thin", "about 123.8 g of PLA (1.24 g/cm³)", "two lines of a 0.4 mm nozzle". language.md: "A model is judged as a print only when the program says it is one, with `set units` or `set bed`". There is no way to say "this is in millimetres" without "this is a print". Expected the unit alone to be a unit. Cost: nothing but noise; the PLA mass of an aluminium plate is wrong by a factor of 2.2 and reads as if it were the mass.

**F3. Engraved letters touching: no warning from `check`, found only by the render's Print row.** `text("MP17-001", 5, weight=1.1, align="center")` cut 0.6 deep. `check` said `no warnings`. The full render's report said: "Thinnest wall | 0.234 mm (0.234 units, 0.5 cells) at (7.7, 11.426, 32.007) in 'part_number', 'blank', through to (7.466, 11.416, 32.014) in 'part_number', 'blank'; under the 1.2 cells the mesh needs to hold a wall". That is the metal left between two engraved letters ("0" and "0"): language.md says the gap between letters is "about a fifth of the size in sans ... less the weight", i.e. 1.0 - 1.1 < 0, so the letters overlap or nearly. `check` warns about "a lettering counter or a slot under a cell", but not about the gap between neighbouring letters of an engraving, which is a sliver of metal. Expected a warning at check time (the language guide itself says this gap is "the one that goes first"). Fix: `spacing=1`. Cost: one full render to find it.

**F4. `--slice tap_probe` frames on the probe, so the far face is out of the picture.** I sliced through the blind hole's probe (7.7 tall, the open part of the hole) to see that the hole stops short of the bottom face. The slices are "framed on it, with a quarter of its size round it", so the frame ended about 2 mm under the drill point and the plate's bottom face (3.25 below the point) was out of frame: the slice shows the drill point but not the metal under it, which was what I wanted to see. Expected: as documented, so this is my choice of probe; but there is no way to slice through a point and keep the whole part's thickness in frame other than a second, taller probe made only to frame a slice. Cost: one full render (11.5 s) whose slices did not answer the question.

**F5. A step made only to be sliced through from the command line is warned as unused.** I added `section_tapped = box(40, 12, 1) | move(-20, 6, 15.5)` and `section_corner = ...` as slabs to aim `--slice` at (F4's fix). `check --brief` said: "warning: 'section_tapped' (line 61) is not part of the output; add it to the model or remove it". language.md: "A step only sliced through is a region", but that holds only when the program's own `set slice` names it; a step meant for `--slice NAME` on the command line is warned, and a program can `set slice` only one. Expected: some way to mark a step as a region (a probe for the pictures) without an assert. Cost: two warnings I have to explain in the report; I left them (they are the slabs) rather than fake an assert. Later `set slice section_tapped` made that one a region; `section_corner` stays warned in every run and is drawn red, UNUSED, on steps.png, which is the sheet saying I forgot something I did not forget.

**F6. The doc's "a hole reads as through when the camera is steep enough to see its floor" did not hold for a plate lying on the floor.** language.md, Lights, cameras and environments: "A focus shot traces the whole model, so a hole under a counterbore reads as through when the camera is steep enough to see its floor (a hole 0.14 across through 0.25 wants an elevation above about 60)". My plan camera is at elevation 90, so it sees straight down every hole, and the four counterbored through holes read as black cores (pixel (46,130) of the first plan beauty), exactly as the blind tapped holes do: the floor under a plate that lies on it is in the plate's shadow, and nothing in the docs says so. Expected: seeing the floor through a hole to read as through. Cost: the first plan render answered nothing about through vs blind.

**F7. No light can make a through hole read in a plan shot of a part lying on the floor; the docs never say so.** I tried the obvious next thing, a second, steep light (`light("down", azimuth=-135, elevation=80, size=1.5, power=0.8)`) to throw light down the through holes onto the floor. lights.png ("DOWN ADDS") showed what it did: a metal top seen from straight above mirrors any light near the vertical, so the whole top went white (254, 254, 254), the pocket's floor and the top became one white, and the steep light reached the drill points of the BLIND tapped holes too: they turned light grey (212, 216, 218) while the THROUGH corner holes stayed darker (146, 150, 151). The reading was inverted: the blind holes looked like the ones you can see the bottom of. A light from below is impossible ("The floor stops a light that comes from under it"). The troubleshooting row for plan shots ("a plan shot's blind holes read as domes or bumps ... with your own lights, put the key at the camera's azimuth less 135") is about relief, not about through vs blind, and no row covers telling them apart. Expected a row: "a through hole and a blind hole read alike from above: the floor under a part is in its shadow; light it from below (raise it, or a glowing panel under it)". Cost: two lighting renders.

**F8. `set ambient` barely darkened a metal top seen from above.** The plan's aluminium top read near white (245 levels at rough 0.5), as white as the light I put under the through holes (253), so the bore read as a white disc on a white plate. language.md: "`set ambient 1.5` scales everything the sky gives the model: the light a surface takes from it and what a metal or a glossy part reflects of it alike, but not the lights". I set `set ambient 0.55` expecting the top to fall well below the glow; it went from 245 to 240 (the pocket floor 218 to 207). Whatever keeps a satin metal top near white from straight above is mostly not the sky's share, or the tone curve hides the change; the docs give the rows "chrome, silver or steel reads near white on top, or in a plan shot" (studio or overcast, "seen from straight above a steel part is near white in either") but no way to make it darker short of a darker metal. Expected: half the sky, a visibly darker top. Cost: one lighting render, reverted.

**F9. `void(corner_rods, plate)` passed with all four corner holes blind: a union of thin probe rods far apart is promised vacuously.** The guide and the reference both teach "a hole that must go through is `void` on a thinner rod reaching past both faces". I made one region of the four rods, `corner_rods = cylinder(2, 20) | move(cb.x, p.t / 2, cb.z) | mirror("x") | mirror("z")` (the language guide mentions "a ring of probe rods", "in copy 3 of the 6 in rods", so a region of copies looked supported). In the probe `dogfood/plate_probe_fail_through.aix` the through part of each corner hole stops 2 above the bottom face (`cylinder(cb.r_hole, 10) | move(0, 2 + 5, 0)`), so every rod passes through 2 mm of metal. `check` said: "asserts: 30 pass", with "assert (line 79) holds: void(corner_rods, plate): every corner hole goes through". The rods are 4 across in a region box 104 × 20 × 64, and language.md says the lattice is "25 points a side" over the region's box: about 4.3 apart in x, so hardly a lattice point falls inside a rod, and the surface walk did not find the 2 mm floor either. Expected: either the promise to fail with "solid at ... in copy 2", or a warning that the region's lattice is coarser than its parts. Nothing said so; this is the one promise the brief is about, and it held of a plate with no through corner holes. Fix: one rod per hole, in a loop. Cost: a false pass, found only because I broke the model on purpose.

**F10. A blind-hole promise built on `height()` becomes an error, not a failure, when the hole goes through.** In `dogfood/plate_probe_fail_blind.aix` the tapped holes are drilled 14 deep through the 12 plate. `check` printed only: "dogfood/plate_probe_fail_blind.aix: line 102: height(): no surface above (-4.5, 15.5)" and exited 1: no sizes, no other assert's verdict, not even which assert or which turn of the loop. The reference does say height() is "An error when nothing is there", so a through hole cannot be promised blind with `height()` without the whole program failing to run. Expected: a number (say -Infinity, as `stands()` reads for no surface) so `assert floor_y > 3` fails with its message and the other promises still report. Cost: the blind promise needs a second form (`inside(cylinder under the hole, plate) == 1`), which I had, but it never ran.

**F11. `set camera plan` turned the sheet's perspective and the whole turntable into plan views.** With `set camera plan` (elevation 90), the sheet's PERSPECTIVE panel is a second top view ("AZIMUTH 0° ELEVATION 90°"), and turntable.png is eight plan views turned 45 degrees apart: no frame of it shows the plate's thickness or a hole's depth. language.md says `set camera hero` "makes that shot the sheet's perspective view and `beauty.png` as well (the front, right and top views are unchanged)"; it does not say the turntable follows the camera's elevation too (the Settings paragraph says `set elevation` turns "the perspective camera used by the sheet, the turntable and the beauty render"). Expected: the turntable to keep a turntable's elevation, and a plan `set camera` not to cost the sheet its only oblique view. The way round is to make the three-quarter the `set camera` shot and pass `--camera plan` for the plan's lighting loop, but then lights.png studies the three-quarter, not the plan; I kept the plan. Cost: the final sheet and turntable carry no oblique view of the model.

**F12. A presentation prop is judged as part of the print.** The backlight panel is a scene object, and the report's Print section now reads "Summary | one piece, watertight; a wall 0.679 mm thin, 1.49 cm³ of support" and "Overhangs | 5.5% of the surface faces down", because the plate's underside overhangs the 4 mm inset panel by 1 mm. The Pieces row is "1": the plate and the panel touch face to face and fuse in the scene's union. Expected: a way to say an object is staging (a light box, a turntable plinth) and leave it out of the physics and print judgement. Cost: two misleading rows I must explain.

**F13. A glossy aluminium wall drew a line along its middle that reads as a step, and no row covers it.** In beauty_three_quarter at `rough=0.35` (render #6) the pocket's back wall was a bright band, a dark line and a bright band; at `rough=0.5` (renders #7 and #12) a dotted line of alternating light and dark pixels ran along the back and right walls just under the lip. Either reads as a ledge partway down the pocket. The slices and two asserts said there is none (below, section 4). The materials paragraph explains why ("From 0.35 to 0.55 ... one traced ray is blended into the blurred sky"; "Above 0.55 a surface reflects only the sky"), and that is how I fixed it (`rough=0.6`), but I had to infer that the one traced ray was the line; the troubleshooting rows nearest it ("a part inside a polished bowl is hard to pick out": satin `rough=0.4`) would have left me at 0.4, inside the band that draws it. Expected a row: "a polished wall shows a line or a dotted line along it: the reflection of the opposite edge, one traced ray a pixel; above 0.55 it is gone". Cost: one finish render.

## 4. Reading the pictures

**What I read off beauty.png that a number showed wrong.** Three readings, all on the way to the final picture, none in it:

1. Render #3, the first plan (the plate on the floor, key and fill): the motor bore showed a grey, softly shaded disc inside a dark crescent of wall, which reads as **a blind bore with a dished floor**. `void(bore_rod, plate)` holds (a 20 mm rod through both faces meets no metal): it goes through, and the "dish" was the studio floor under the plate with the key's light falling on part of it through the bore (lights.png's KEY ADDS, once the panel was under the plate, showed the key's light falling on the same lower-right part of the bore).
2. Render #4, the plan with a steep second light: **the blind tapped holes read as the shallow ones, the ones you can see the bottom of** (light grey, (212, 216, 218)), and the counterbored through holes as the deep ones (dark grey cores, (146, 150, 151)). The numbers say the opposite: the tapped holes' floors are at y = 3.25, 8.75 down and 3.25 short of the far face (`height()`, 4 times), and each corner hole goes through (`void` of a rod per hole, 4 times). The reading was inverted.
3. Renders #6, #7 and #12, the three-quarter: the pocket's back wall drew a bright band, a dark line and a bright band (rough 0.35), then a dotted line just under the lip (0.5), which reads as **a ledge partway down the pocket**. The Z = 15.5 slice shows one 5 mm wall, and two asserts say so: `height(plate, 25, -25.7)` equals the floor (difference 0) and `height(plate, 25, -26.3)` equals the top (1.4e-10).

And one the other way round, a number that the picture corrected: `void(corner_rods, plate)` held with all four corner holes blind (F9); the backlit plan of that probe (render #15) shows their cores dark, (33, 39, 44), where the real plate's are blue.

In the final pictures I checked every feature against a number and found nothing read wrong: through holes blue in the plan (4 + 1 `void` promises), blind holes black (4 `height` floors of 3.25, 4 `inside` == 1 under them), counterbores a ring and a shoulder (4 shoulders at 5.5 down to 0.0012), the pocket recessed with its floor at 7 (1.4e-10). The pocket's depth against the plate I read off the three-quarter as "a little under half the 12 mm front edge"; it is 5 of 12, 0.42.

**How many renders the lighting took, and the finish.**

- **The plan shot's lighting: 6 renders** (#3, #4, #5, #8, #9, #10), right at the 6th. What I tried: (1) the docs' plan rule, key at the camera's azimuth less 135 (`-135`, 55 up), with a front-right fill: relief right, pocket recessed and no domes, but through and blind alike black; (2) a steep second light at 80 degrees to reach the floor through the through holes: the metal top went white, the pocket vanished, and the blind holes lit up instead (inverted); (3) a glowing white panel under the plate, a light table: through holes showed light, blind holes stayed black, but the bore was a white disc on a white top (252 against 245); (4) the panel's glow to 1.6 for a bloom: no change, the top is as bright as the glow; (5) `set ambient 0.55` to darken what the top mirrors: 245 to 240, reverted (F8); (6) the panel blue (`#8cc4ff`): the through holes read at once as light coming up through the plate. So **the plan needed a light that is not a `light()`**: the glowing panel is the only thing I found that tells a through hole from a blind one from straight above, since no light can come from under the floor and a steep light lights both kinds of hole.
- **The three-quarter's lighting: no change** (the same key and fill); its camera took 2 renders (#6 at 35 degrees up, #12 at 45, to see into the counterbores).
- **The finish: 3 renders**, one per roughness (#6 judged `rough=0.35` on the three-quarter: polished walls banded; #7 `0.5`: a dotted line along the walls and a paler pocket floor in the plan; #13 `0.6`: clean). `0.6` costs the three-quarter one cue: at 0.5 the corner holes' walls mirrored the blue panel and showed the through hole from 45 degrees up; at 0.6 they reflect only the sky and are dark, so through-ness is read in the plan alone.

**The troubleshooting rows I met.**

- *"a plan shot's blind holes read as domes or bumps"* (the guide's and the skill's tables; language.md's "a plan shot with its own lights puts the key at the camera's azimuth less 135"). I applied it before meeting the symptom, from the first plan render. It worked for what it says: the pocket and every hole read as recessed, never as a dome. **It did not get me to through vs blind**, which is the brief's bar, and it does not claim to.
- *"chrome, silver or steel reads near white on its top faces, or all over in a plan shot"*. Met: the aluminium top was 231 to 245 from straight above in every render. The row explains it ("seen from straight above a steel part is near white in either") but its fixes (a lower camera, a darker metal, the studio) are not open to a plan shot of a bright metal; that whiteness is what made a white backlight unreadable. Did not get me out; the blue panel did.
- language.md's *"a hole under a counterbore reads as through when the camera is steep enough to see its floor"*. Met and did not hold: the floor under a part lying on it is in the part's shadow, so a through hole seen from straight above is as black as a blind one (F6).
- *"a part inside a polished bowl is hard to pick out"* (satin `rough=0.4`): the nearest row to the pocket walls' false ledge; it would have left me inside the band that draws it (F13). The materials paragraph's "above 0.55 ... only the sky" is what fixed it.
- *"a polished engraving is faint, or sparkles"*: watched for, not met: the engraving read dark (122) on the top (244) at every roughness I tried.
- **No row** for: through and blind holes reading alike from above (F6, F7); a steep light washing a metal top (F7); `set ambient` not darkening a metal top (F8); the dotted line on a glossy wall (F13).

**Did the rows about plan shots and holes get me there?** Half way. The plan-shot key rule gave the relief at the first render, and the slices (`--slice` on a slab through the hole row) were the truth about blind holes, as the guide promises. What the bar asks, "which holes go through and which are blind" from the pictures alone, no row and no light reached: it took a glowing panel under the plate, found by reasoning about what the floor can show, and a colour to separate it from the white of a metal top.

## 5. What worked well

- **The promises in loops.** `assert (line 104) holds 4 times: floor_y > 3 is 3.25 > 3 each time` is exactly the line I want; a failure prints each judgement with where the metal is and which step gave it ("solid at (48.17, 1, 29.33) in 'blank'"), so the four failing holes named themselves.
- **`height()` for floors.** The pocket floor, the counterbore shoulders and the drill points came out to 1.4e-10, 0.0012 and exactly 3.25: the brief's "a blind hole's floor" and "the pocket's depth" are one query each.
- **`lights.png`.** "DOWN ADDS" showed in one frame why the steep light failed (the whole top white), and NO LIGHT showed the panel carrying the through holes on its own; without it I would have guessed at the steep light's effect from one combined picture.
- **`--beauty-only` with `--camera`**: 11 to 16 s at grid 256 for the one shot in full quality, and `--out` kept the three-quarter loop from deleting the plan's files.
- **`--slice` on a slab region.** A 40 × 12 × 1 slab along the hole row put the Z plane through both blind holes and the pocket in one picture; the language guide's "Sliced through a probe or a cutter, the planes go through its volume" is what told me to try it.
- **`callouts.png`** labelled the disc in the bore BACKLIGHT: the picture says itself that what shows through the hole is under the plate.
- **The cut thumbnails** on steps.png draw each cutter as the solid it removes, so the counterbored cutter and the drill-pointed tap were checkable before any beauty render.

## 6. Three changes that would have helped most

1. **A way to light through a part, and a row that says why it is needed.** From straight above, a through hole in a part lying on the floor is as black as a blind one, a steep light lights the blind holes' floors and washes a metal top white, and no `light()` can come from under the floor. Give the studio a light table (`set environment lightbox`, a floor that glows under the model) or let a point light under a raised part light the floor; and add the row: "through and blind holes read alike in a plan shot: light from below; a glowing panel under the part (`material(..., glow=1.6)` in a colour the top is not) shows the through holes". Correct language.md's "a hole under a counterbore reads as through when the camera is steep enough to see its floor", which is not true of a part on the floor.
2. **`void()` must not pass a region of thin copies vacuously, and `height()` must not stop the program.** Judge each piece of a region (each copy of a `mirror`, `ring` or union) on its own lattice, or warn when the lattice is coarser than the region's parts; four 4 mm rods in a 104 × 64 box held "every corner hole goes through" of a plate whose corner holes were blind. And let `height()` over nothing return a number (-Infinity) so "the hole is blind" fails as an assert with its message instead of halting `check` with "height(): no surface above (-4.5, 15.5)".
3. **Let a program say what is not the part.** A unit without a print (`set units mm` brought a Print section with PLA grams to an aluminium plate), a staging object (the light panel made the plate "need" 1.49 cm³ of support and fused into its piece count), a slicing slab (warned "not part of the output" and drawn red), and a plan `set camera` that does not take the sheet's only perspective view and the whole turntable to 90 degrees. One marker, a `region` or `stage` tag on a step, and a turntable that keeps its own elevation, would have removed four of the thirteen points above.
